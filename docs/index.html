Four classes: glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor, no tumor. When it finds a tumor, a second model outlines it. Both run inside this page, so the image never leaves your device. It was trained and tested with near-duplicate images removed, which is why its accuracy is 96%, not the 99% that leaked benchmarks report.

Why 96%, not 99%

Public brain-tumor sets reuse near-identical slices of the same patients in training and test. A model that has seen the twin of a test image scores high without recognising the tumor.

30%of the test images in version 2 of the popular Kaggle set, released as de-duplicated, still have a near-identical twin in its training folder (362 of the 400 no-tumor images).
107Kaggle test images appear byte for byte in the training split of BRISC 2025, so BRISC is not an independent test for models trained on the Kaggle set.
22%of test gliomas from other hospitals were recognised by a model scoring 95.9% overall: every training glioma came from one source, and the model learned the source.
48% → 91%accuracy on T1 and T2 slices once training includes those sequences; models trained on the standard sets miss most gliomas imaged that way.

So every training image with a twin (correlation ≥ 0.90) in any test set was removed, the model and the “no tumor” threshold were chosen on the BRISC test, and the Kaggle test was scored once with those choices fixed.

Model card

EfficientNetV2-S (ImageNet-21k pretrained), 384×384 input, fine-tuned on 9,026 slices from the Kaggle set v2, BRISC 2025 and a T1 / contrast T1 / T2 collection (Feltrin), after removing twins of all test images. Weights stored in 16-bit floats for the browser (40 MB) and computed in 32-bit, which gives the same answers as the original model on both test sets; 8-bit quantisation was tried and rejected because it dropped accuracy to 85%.

Over three training seeds the same recipe gives 95.94 ± 0.23% on the Kaggle test; averaging the three models gives 96.4% and misses 3 of 1,200 tumors. This page runs one of them to keep the download small.

Tumor outline. A second, smaller model (U-Net with an EfficientNet-B0 encoder, 320×320, 12 MB) draws the red outline. It was trained on 3,507 slices with tumor masks drawn by radiologists in BRISC 2025, plus normal slices with empty masks, after the same twin removal (984 of the 3,933 BRISC segmentation training images had a twin in a test set). On the 860 BRISC test tumors it overlaps the radiologists’ outline with a Dice score of 0.86 (meningioma 0.93, pituitary 0.87, glioma 0.76, whose borders are the least clear) and finds 98.9% of the tumors. It runs only when the classifier reports a tumor; on normal slices it drew an outline on 1 of 140 BRISC images and on 12% of Kaggle images from other sources.

Limits. Single 2D slices, not volumes. No pituitary images in T1 or T2 were available for training. Twin removal catches near-identical slices, not every slice of the same patient. Images from scanners or protocols unlike these sets may be misclassified with high confidence.
